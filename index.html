Decide and finance

Before anything is ordered · weeks 0 to 2

Start from the workload, not the hardware. Training wants the tightest fabric you can afford (InfiniBand or a lossless dual-plane Ethernet) and a real shared storage tier for checkpoints. Inference wants smaller GPU groupings, north-south bandwidth and elasticity; it can often run on local NVMe alone. A mixed cluster needs a decision on how it is partitioned, static or elastic, because that decision drives the scheduler and the network design.

Pick a scale unit and repeat it. The quotes on file price 32 nodes (256 GPUs); the Civo design treats 64 nodes (512 GPUs) as one self-contained scale unit with no spine layer, and adds a spine only when a second unit arrives. Building in identical units keeps the leaf/spine maths, the rack elevations and the cabling identical each time.

Know your money before you know your OEM. Order forms are valid for 14 days, are prepaid, and pricing can move before shipment. A financed build needs the facility drawdown and compliance checks done before the OEM order goes in; Civo budget two weeks for that alone. Export compliance is a hard gate: every OEM will require an end-user certification for advanced ICs, naming the destination country and end use.

Rule of thumb from the quotes. At 32 nodes the servers are 52 to 56% of total cost. Fabric plus optics is 14 to 16%. Storage is 7 to 11%. Managed services over three years is 16 to 18%. Racks and power are about 1%.
Checklist
01

Site and colocation

Contract before the OEM order · lead time 4 to 12 weeks for high-density space

Power density is the first question the colo will ask. An air-cooled HGX B300 node draws around 13 kW under load; a 48U rack holding two of them plus switches needs two 34.5 kW three-phase PDUs (240/415 V, 60 A). The Lenovo B200 spec puts a six-node liquid-cooled rack at 69.6 kW nominal max and 1,813 A total across 32 nodes. Most colos cannot deliver this in a standard hall, so ask for a high-density suite and get the per-rack kW in writing.

Air or liquid decides the site. Air-cooled B300 racks at four nodes per rack need heavy cold-aisle containment and roughly 52 kW of heat rejection per rack. Direct liquid cooling packs eight nodes into a rack with an in-rack 250 kW CDU, but the facility must supply a chilled-water loop, DN65 primary connections, a coolant fill and a water-quality regime.

Floor loading and access. A loaded 48U rack weighs 850 to 900 kg; a 10U GPU server needs a lift-assist cart or the OEM will refuse on-site service. Confirm raised-floor rating, ramp and lift capacity, and a staging area where 32 crates can sit.

Cross-connects and carriers. Budget at least two diverse 100G or 400G carrier circuits for ingress, plus cross-connects to any cloud on-ramp your customers will pull data from. These are not in any of the OEM quotes.

02

Compute platform

Order at week 2 · 8-week manufacturing and logistics lead time

All the B300 options are the same 8-GPU HGX baseboard in a different chassis. What changes is the host side (Intel Xeon 6 6776P or AMD EPYC Turin), memory (2.3 to 3 TB), the NIC arrangement, support tier and price. NVIDIA DGX B300 is the reference, with NVIDIA's own support and mandatory partner installation. Dell XE9780 adds iDRAC, secure component verification and ProSupport Plus with 4-hour mission-critical response. Penguin's Wistron HGX is the lowest-cost OEM route with Penguin-integrated racks. Supermicro and the KR9288 in the Civo design are alternatives without a price on file.

Per-node configuration to hold constant across vendors: 8 × B300 (288 GB HBM3e each), 2 CPUs, 24 to 32 DIMMs of DDR5-6400, 8 × 3.84 TB NVMe scratch, 2 × 960 GB M.2 boot in RAID 1, 8 × ConnectX-8 (one per GPU) for the compute fabric, and separate NICs for storage and front-end. Power supplies: 6+6 redundant at 3,200 W, or 12 × 5,500 W on the Wistron chassis.

Where the market sits (September 2026): HGX B300 nodes are being offered between $558K and $722K per server depending on host CPU, memory (2 TB vs 3 TB), NIC set and support tier, with allocations from 32 to 300 units and lead times from 3 weeks to 16 weeks after NVIDIA and end-user approval. The full list, with lead times, is in the supply market section and can be selected in the builder.

Ask each OEM to quote the identical spec sheet. The three quotes on file differ by $88,683 per node between lowest and highest, which is $2.8M across 32 nodes, before the difference in support terms.

03

Network fabric

Order at week 2 · switches usually in stock · install from week 7, ahead of servers

Four networks, physically separate. Compute (east-west GPU traffic, one 800G port per GPU, rail-optimised), storage (one 200G or 400G NIC per node, kept off the compute fabric so checkpoint bursts cannot trigger PFC pause), front-end or in-band (logins, control plane, ingress, inference endpoints), and out-of-band (BMC/IPMI on 1 GbE, plus console servers for every switch).

InfiniBand or Ethernet. NVIDIA Quantum-3 XDR (Q3400, 144 ports of 800G) with UFM is the highest-performance, single-vendor route; the Supermicro BOM uses 4 leaf + 3 spine Q3400 for 32 nodes at a 1:1 down:up ratio. Arista 7060X6 (32 × 800G) with CloudVision, or NVIDIA Spectrum-4 SN5610 (64 × 800G) with Cumulus, give a lossless RoCEv2 fabric that is vendor-agnostic and slightly cheaper; the Civo design runs it as parallel A and B planes so one plane can be drained for maintenance.

Optics are a line item people forget. At 32 nodes the transceivers alone are $1.0M to $3.3M depending on fabric, and both Penguin quotes carry a $400,000 cable placeholder "TBD once datacenter layouts are confirmed". Structured fibre (MPO-12 APC single-mode, 5 to 15 m runs, 1:2 breakouts for spine) is only knowable once you have rack elevations.

Reference port maths at 128 nodes (Tech Directcore, Quantum-3 XDR, 1:1 non-blocking): 16 leaf + 8 spine Q3400, 1,024 single-port 800G transceivers on the nodes, 512 twin-port 1600G on the leaves toward nodes and 1,152 toward spines, 1,024 node-to-leaf and 576 leaf-to-spine 30 m fibres, 2 UFM appliances with a 1K+ HCA subscription. In-band and storage on 8 × SN5600 (4 GPU-side leaves, 2 storage-side leaves, 2 spines). OOB on 19 × SN2201. Edge: 2 × Juniper MX204 with 4 × 100GE, 2 × QFX5120-48Y, 2 × Fortigate 1800F, a /24 announced on your own ASN, operator-level anti-DDoS.

Support and licences are recurring. The Arista quote bundles 36 months of CloudVision and A-Care per switch; NVIDIA switches carry 3-year Business Standard support. Put renewal dates in the operating calendar from day one.

04

Racks, power and cabling

Rack and stack from week 7 · cabling weeks 9 to 11

The rack elevation is the master document. The Supermicro draft BOM lays out an 11-cabinet air row for 32 nodes: 8 compute racks (4 × 8U nodes each at U8, U17, U26, U35), one leaf rack (4 × Q3400), one spine rack (3 × Q3400 plus the BlueField-3 uplinks), and one Ethernet/OOB rack (2 × SN2201 at U47/U48, 2 × SN5610). The liquid-cooled version collapses to 7 cabinets with 8 nodes and a 250 kW CDU per compute rack. Penguin's quotes use 2 nodes per rack across 16 GPU racks plus spine, leaf and admin racks.

Per rack you need: a 48U, 750 to 800 mm wide, 1200 to 1400 mm deep cabinet with a shock package and bolt-down kit; two switched and monitored 3-phase PDUs (34.5 kW each for air, 52.7 kVA for DLC); zero-U PDU brackets; blanking panels for every empty U; cage nuts; cable-management straps; and locking C19-C20 power cords, colour-coded per feed. Rack integration and burn-in at the factory means the rack arrives crated with tilt and shock indicators.

Weight check. The Lenovo spec gives 869 kg per six-node rack against an 1,829 kg rating. A four-node air B300 rack with 10U chassis lands in the same range; confirm the colo floor rating before delivery.

05

Storage

Optional at first order · sized at low-level design

Local NVMe covers more than people expect. Each node carries about 30 TB of scratch, so a 32-node cluster has close to 1 PB of local flash. Inference and per-job staged training can run on that alone.

Add a shared tier when you have shared datasets, frequent multi-node checkpointing or model artefact serving at scale. The quotes on file price roughly 1 PB usable of VAST at $2.78M including three years of subscription and service, and Dell PowerScale F710 (10 nodes, 1.54 PB raw, plus two Z9664F switches) at $4.61M including hardware and software maintenance and deployment. WEKA is the third common choice. Whichever you pick, protection is platform snapshots only unless you buy backup separately.

06

Software and cluster management

Decide before the LLD · installed during burn-in, weeks 11 to 12

Head nodes are real hardware. Penguin quote five 1U Altus servers (dual EPYC 9255, 384 GB, ConnectX-8) for provisioning, scheduling, login and monitoring, plus a ClusterWare subscription licensed per node and per GPU for three years ($1.98M at 256 GPUs). The alternative is NVIDIA Base Command Manager, or an open-source stack (Slurm or Kubernetes, Prometheus and DCGM exporter, NetBox, an image server), which costs engineering time rather than licences.

Define before handover: the scheduler and how training and inference share it, the observability stack for GPU and fabric telemetry, the multi-tenancy and security model, the OS image and driver baseline, and the day-2 operating model with its SLA. For IB, UFM appliances (2, with 3-year subscription) manage the subnet; for Arista, CloudVision does the same job.

07

Deployment and managed services

Deploy weeks 7 to 12 · operations from handover

Someone has to rack, cable, configure, burn in and then run it. Penguin price a one-off "deploy 256 GPUs" professional service at $646K and a managed service (NBD break-fix, remote support, engagement management, dashboards, storage and network, cluster management) at $1.96M per year. Over three years that is $6.5M, or 16 to 18% of the total. The alternative is an in-house team of roughly four to six people covering fabric, storage, platform and on-call, plus the OEM hardware support you already bought.

Support tiers to line up: servers (NBD vs 4-hour on-site), switches (NBD replacement with same-day ship), storage (4-hour on-site is standard in the Dell quote), head nodes (3-year platform support), and the CDU (annual maintenance and quarterly water testing for five years in the Lenovo spec).

08

Timeline, acceptance and handover

14 weeks from facility drawdown to bare-metal handover

Two parallel procurement tracks, one overlapped install. Servers carry an 8-week lead time; switches are usually in stock, so network installation starts at week 7 before the GPUs arrive. Rack and stack continues as servers land, then cabling, network configuration and burn-in run in sequence. Customer UAT and acceptance run together in the final fortnight, ending with handover at week 14.

At 1,024 GPUs the clock is longer. Tech Directcore's Gantt starts only at contract signature plus deposit (W0), carries a 12 to 14 week allocation and procurement window for 1,024 B300s, runs data-hall readiness W1 to W12 and cabling W10 to W12 in parallel, racks and stacks at W14 to W15, builds and burns in the IB fabric at W15 to W16, and targets first availability around W16.

Acceptance means numbers, not a walk-through. Every GPU passes DCGM diagnostics; NCCL all-reduce across the full cluster hits the expected bus bandwidth; every fabric link runs error-free at full rate for 48 hours; storage meets its validated read and write throughput; every PDU, BMC and console is reachable; a full node loss and a full leaf loss are simulated.

Supply market, September 2026

Per-server offers for HGX B300 8-GPU nodes and GB300 NVL72, with quantity and lead time as quoted. Select any row in the builder above.

Read the lead time with the price. The spread on a like-for-like HGX B300 node is $558K to $720K, but the cheap end often carries "reconfirmation required" or "assembly and CPU inclusion to be clarified", and every allocation is gated on NVIDIA and end-user approval. A firm 8 to 9 week delivery at $605K beats an indicative $565K that may not exist by the time the PO is cut.

GB300 NVL72 is a different unit of purchase. One rack is 72 GPUs at $6.02M, about $83.6K per GPU against $70K to $80K per GPU for HGX B300 nodes, and it needs liquid cooling and 120 kW+ per rack. Switch trays are sold separately on a full-lot basis.
